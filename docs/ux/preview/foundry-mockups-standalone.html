The calls I made, and why

These are decisions, not options. I’ve already weighed them — but if one looks wrong for your business, say so and I’ll rethink it.

  • Most of your visitors arrive on a phone, mid-problem, so the emergency route is one tap from every page rather than buried in a menu.
  • Estimate requests are stored rather than emailed, because an email you miss is a job you lose and a list you can work through is not.
  • Services get their own page each, so searches for a specific job find a specific page instead of your home page.
  • Reviews sit next to the call button, since that is the moment someone decides whether to trust you.

What I’ve deliberately left out

Knowing what a project isn’t matters as much as knowing what it is. None of this is an oversight — ask for any of it and I’ll fold it in.

  • Online payment. Trade work is quoted before it is priced, so taking card details up front would cost you jobs rather than win them.
  • A blog. It is a commitment to write, and an empty blog reads worse than no blog at all.
  • Live chat. You are under a sink most of the day, and an unanswered chat widget damages trust more than not having one.
  • Customer accounts. Nobody creates a login to book a plumber once. If repeat servicing becomes a real part of the business, that changes.

I’d also recommend

These aren’t upsells or a checklist. They’re the things I’d push for if we were sitting down together, because businesses like yours usually get real value from them.

One thing only you can decide

Everything else I’ve already decided. If you’d rather not weigh in, skip it and I’ll use my judgement — that’s a perfectly good answer.

Should estimate requests be something only you can see?

If they are private, the site needs a way for you to sign in, and that changes how the whole thing is put together. If they are not, anyone with the link could read your customers’ addresses — which I would not do to you.
